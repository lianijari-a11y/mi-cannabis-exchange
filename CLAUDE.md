# MI Cannabis Exchange — Build Brief for Claude Code

Read this before writing code. This is a **separate business from LaneMatch** (a freight-matching platform built elsewhere) — do not reuse its compliance model, only its engineering conventions.

---

## 1. What this is

A wholesale marketplace connecting five Michigan-licensed cannabis roles:

- **Grower** — cultivates flower, posts inventory
- **Processor** — makes concentrates/edibles/vapes, posts inventory
- **Retailer** — buys wholesale inventory to sell at retail
- **Broker** — the platform's own intermediary; mediates every deal
- **Transporter** — Michigan Secure Transporter-licensed carrier; hauls an accepted deal from Grower/Processor to Retailer once the retailer has accepted the seller's invoice

A Grower, Processor, or Broker can post an inventory listing. It becomes **immediately visible** to every Retailer (a bulletin-board model, not a platform-assigned match). A Retailer can accept a listing as posted, or counter-offer on price and/or payment terms (cash vs. 30-day terms are both negotiable). The Grower/Processor can then accept, reject, or counter back — this can go back and forth an arbitrary number of rounds. The Broker sees **every** negotiation across the whole platform, on both sides, in real identity — Retailer and Grower/Processor never see each other's identity, even after a deal closes.

Once a deal is accepted, fulfillment kicks off: the seller uploads an invoice, the retailer reviews it and accepts, which triggers transporter selection (the platform's preferred transporter is pre-selected, but the retailer can pick any other approved transporter) and creates a trackable shipment. See §3's 4th decision and §4's Transporter section.

## 2. The compliance wrinkle — read before touching identity/broker code

This was flagged, not silently decided, when the product shape was defined:

- Michigan requires **METRC seed-to-sale manifests** for any real transfer of licensed product, and a manifest names actual licensees (license number, business name) on both sides.
- It is **not established** that "broker" is a recognized standalone license category for wholesale cannabis transfers the way it is for real estate or (per FMCSA) freight — the Marihuana Regulation and Taxation Act (MRTA) and Medical Marihuana Facilities Licensing Act (MMFLA) license Growers, Processors, Retailers, Secure Transporters, Safety Compliance Facilities, Microbusinesses, etc. Whether an entity that never holds/transports product but sits between every deal and knows both identities needs its own license, or needs to operate under a Retailer/licensed-transporter's authority, is a real open legal question.
- **Do not resolve this in code or in this document.** The app is built so the Broker role has full visibility (matching the confirmed product decision), but the actual legal structure the platform owner operates under — a licensed entity, a partnership with one, a pure software-only "provides negotiation tooling, a licensed transporter completes the physical/METRC transfer" model, etc. — needs review by a Michigan cannabis attorney before this goes live with real product. Flag this to the human if asked to build real METRC integration or anything that implies the platform itself is executing a transfer.
- Until that's resolved, treat `Deal` records in this app as **negotiated agreements**, not completed legal transfers — the actual license-to-license transfer and METRC manifest happen outside this software, initiated by the Broker.
- The same caveat applies to `Shipment`/invoice records added for fulfillment: an uploaded invoice, an accepted invoice, and a transporter's "delivered" status update are **operational tracking inside this app**, not a substitute for the real METRC manifest and physical secure-transport paperwork a licensed transporter is separately obligated to keep. Don't build anything that implies this app's shipment record *is* the legal transfer record.

## 3. The three product decisions that shape the architecture

1. **Blind marketplace, identity never revealed between Grower/Processor and Retailer** — not before a deal, not after. Every API endpoint scoped to those two roles must never join to or expose the counterparty's `User` row. Enforce this at the query layer, not just in the UI — the UI hiding a field is not enough if the API response includes it.
2. **Broker sees every deal, platform-wide** — Brokers are not independent competing agents each with their own book of business; every broker user can see every active negotiation and every closed deal, with both real identities. This models the Broker as the marketplace operator's own staff/agents, not third-party brokers competing on the platform.
3. **First-to-accept / no platform-assigned matching** — a listing is visible to every Retailer simultaneously; nothing in the matching/feed logic should rank or gate listings based on the platform's own business interest. (This mirrors LaneMatch's load-board reasoning even though the legal basis here is different — it's just good practice for a bulletin-board model.)
4. **Transporter identity is real, not anonymized, and the retailer picks the transporter.** Confirmed with the human when the fulfillment flow was added: once a deal moves to shipping, the Transporter sees both the Grower/Processor's and Retailer's real business name and address (same trust tier as the Broker — needed for pickup/delivery logistics and matches how MI's Secure Transporter licensing already implies manifest-level detail on both parties). This does **not** loosen decision #1 — Grower/Processor and Retailer still never see *each other's* identity; they each separately see the Transporter's real identity, because a shipping carrier isn't the counterparty the blind-marketplace rule is protecting. Transporter selection happens when the retailer accepts the seller's invoice: the platform's preferred transporter (`User.preferredTransporter`, admin-set) is pre-selected, but any other approved Transporter account can be chosen instead — this is a self-serve role like the others, not a closed vendor list.

If you find yourself building something that would let the platform pick which Retailer gets a listing, or would let a Grower/Processor and Retailer see each other's identity, stop and flag it.

## 4. Roles and core functionality

### Grower / Processor
- Profile: business name, state license number, license type, license verification status (set by Admin)
- Post inventory listings: strain/product name, category (flower / concentrate / vape / edible / pre-roll / other), THC%, quantity + unit (lbs for flower/processed weight, liters for liquid concentrate/oil), price per unit, payment terms offered (cash / net-30 / negotiable), expiration, photos and videos
- **AI-assisted quick upload**: sellers post multiple times a day, often for very different products, so the listing form has a "paste your notes, structure with AI" step (`lib/ai-listing.ts`, Anthropic API) that turns raw pasted text into a pre-filled draft of the fields above. The seller always reviews and submits the draft themselves — the model drafts, it never posts a listing on its own (same AI-governance posture as the rest of this brief: recommend, don't act autonomously).
- View incoming offers/counters on their listings, respond (accept / reject / counter)
- Listings list, sortable/filterable by status
- **Post-acceptance**: once a deal is accepted, upload an invoice (image/PDF) against it. The retailer's side stays anonymized (still shown only as their anon handle) until/unless a shipment exists, at which point the seller also sees the real Transporter's name and the shipment status timeline (see §3 decision #4)

### Transporter
- Profile: business name, state license number/type (Secure Transporter), license verification status (set by Admin) — same self-serve signup + Admin approval gate as Grower/Processor/Retailer
- Dashboard: every shipment assigned to them, with the deal's real Grower/Processor and Retailer business name + address (needed for pickup/delivery), product/quantity/price/terms
- Update shipment status one step at a time: `assigned → picked_up → in_transit → delivered`, optionally with a note each step; delivery requires uploading a proof-of-delivery photo/PDF
- Does **not** see the negotiation history or listing feed — only shipments assigned to them

### Retailer
- Browse a feed of **all active listings** platform-wide, from every Grower/Processor/Broker poster — poster shown only as an anonymous handle (e.g. "Grower #A17"), never a real business name
- Filter/sort by category, strain, THC%, price, terms
- **Presentation is standardized**: every listing renders through one shared product-card component (`components/retailer/listing-card.tsx`) regardless of how rough the seller's original upload was — same layout, same badge placement, same typography — so the feed reads as one consistent catalog, not a pile of ad hoc posts
- Accept a listing as-is, or counter-offer (price and/or terms) — negotiation is a back-and-forth thread, anonymized on both sides
- See their own open/closed negotiation threads
- **Post-acceptance**: review the seller's uploaded invoice, then accept it and pick a transporter (preferred one pre-selected, any other approved transporter selectable) — this creates the shipment. From here the seller stays anonymized as always, but the chosen Transporter's real name and shipment status timeline are shown

### Broker
- Platform-wide dashboard: every active negotiation and every closed deal, in real identity on both sides (business name, license info)
- No action needed to "join" a deal — visibility is automatic and total, per decision #2 above
- Deal/negotiation status at a glance; this view is explicitly mobile-first (per the original brief: "broker sees all interactions on his phone app")
- Deal cards also show invoice/shipment status and the assigned transporter once fulfillment starts

### Admin
- License verification queue: review a submitted license number/type per user, approve/reject — this now gates Transporters too, not just Grower/Processor/Retailer
- User list/status
- Toggle which Transporter account is the platform's preferred default (`User.preferredTransporter`)

## 5. Negotiation state machine

One thread per (listing, retailer) pair. Each round is one of: `offer` (initial, implicit from the listing itself), `counter` (either side), `accept`, `reject`. A thread is `open` until an `accept` (→ creates a `Deal`) or a `reject` from either side, or the listing/thread expires. Either side can counter as many times as they want while open. All of this is visible to the Broker in real identity; visible to the two negotiating parties only in anonymized form.

Post-acceptance fulfillment is a second, simpler state machine on the `Deal`: no invoice → invoice uploaded → invoice accepted (creates the `Shipment`). The `Shipment` itself steps one-way through `assigned → picked_up → in_transit → delivered`, advanced only by the assigned Transporter (`lib/shipments.ts` `advanceShipmentStatus`, using `NEXT_SHIPMENT_STATUS` — no skipping ahead or reverting).

## 6. Data model

See `web/prisma/schema.prisma` — `User` (role: grower/processor/retailer/broker/transporter/admin, license fields, `address`/`city`/`state`/`zip` for grower/processor/retailer, `preferredTransporter` flag), `Listing`, `ListingMedia`, `OfferThread` + `OfferRound`, `Deal` (now with `invoiceUrl`/`invoiceUploadedAt`/`invoiceAcceptedAt`), `Shipment` + `ShipmentEvent`. The anonymization boundary is enforced in `web/src/lib/listings.ts` and `web/src/lib/offers.ts` — those modules are the only places allowed to query across the Grower/Processor ↔ Retailer boundary, and they must never return the counterparty's real `User` fields to a non-broker, non-admin, non-assigned-transporter caller. `web/src/lib/shipments.ts` owns the invoice/shipment logic and is the one place allowed to resolve a Deal's real seller/retailer identity out to the assigned Transporter.

## 7. Stack

Next.js 16, Prisma (SQLite for local dev — zero external setup; swap to Postgres via `DATABASE_URL` when a hosted DB is provisioned, same as LaneMatch's Supabase setup), NextAuth v5 (credentials), Tailwind v4, TypeScript. Local disk storage for photo/video uploads during development (`public/uploads/`) — swap for real object storage (S3/Supabase Storage/Cloudinary) before production; note this in code as a follow-up, don't silently pretend it's production-ready. Anthropic API (`ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`) powers the AI quick-upload draft feature (`lib/ai-listing.ts`) — unset by default, feature falls back to a plain "fill in by hand" message.

## 8. Deferred — circle back before real launch

- Real object storage/CDN for media (currently local disk)
- `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` not set by default — AI quick-upload structuring is a no-op fallback until the platform owner adds a key
- METRC manifest generation/integration — **blocked on the legal question in §2**
- SMS/push/email notifications — §10 added in-app-only notifications (offer viewed, countered, accepted/rejected); Brokers still get nothing pushed to them outside the app
- Payment/invoicing automation for net-30 terms (this app tracks the *agreed* terms, it doesn't move money)
- Real license verification (currently a manual Admin approve/reject, no FMCSA-SAFER-style automated check exists for MI cannabis licenses the way it does for DOT authority)
- Legal review of the Broker's licensing/liability structure (§2) — do not build around an assumption here without that review
- No live GPS/location tracking for shipments yet — status is transporter-reported (assigned/picked up/in transit/delivered), not automatic. A real ELD/GPS integration is a natural Phase 2, same spirit as LaneMatch's Motive/Samsara integration
- No address validation/geocoding on the signup address fields — free text for now
- Invoice/POD documents are stored on local disk like listing media — same production storage caveat applies
- `SplitContract` (§10) fulfillment isn't wired into `Shipment`/Transporter — settled toll-processing contracts have no tracked pickup/transport step and no METRC-equivalent trail, same underlying gap as §2

## 10. Market intelligence, alerts, and toll-processing (added 2026-08-16)

Built after the initial build brief, evaluated against a reference prototype shared by the human — see §9's note on what was deliberately *not* ported from it.

- **Market Pulse** (`lib/market.ts`) — average asking price by category across active listings, shown on the retailer feed and every seller dashboard. Computed live from this platform's own data, not a third-party report.
- **Sold comps** (`lib/market.ts`) — recent closed `Deal`s in the same category, shown on a listing's detail page.
- **Seller rating** (`lib/market.ts`) — a star score derived from what fraction of a seller's past deals actually reached `delivered`. New sellers with no closed deals get no score rather than a fabricated default. On the retailer side this is resolved by `sellerRatingForListing(listingId)`, which never exposes the real `sellerId` to the caller — same anonymization boundary as everywhere else.
- **License expiration alerts** (`lib/admin.ts`) — Admin dashboard flags any licensed user (`User.licenseExpiry`) expiring within 90 days, or already expired.
- **Watchlist** — a retailer can track a listing without making an offer on it (`Watchlist` model, `lib/watchlist.ts`), with a dedicated `/retailer/watchlist` page.
- **Offer view tracking + in-app notifications** — `OfferThread` now tracks a view count and last-viewed timestamp per side (`lib/offers.ts`'s `recordThreadView`); the *other* party gets a throttled (max once per 10 min) notification that their offer was viewed, plus notifications on counter/accept/reject. This is the achievable slice of §8's deferred "SMS/push to Brokers" item — **in-app only**, no SMS/push/email, surfaced via a bell icon in `components/notification-bell.tsx` on every portal.
- **Auto-generated invoice** (`lib/invoice.ts`) — an invoice used to require the Grower/Processor to manually upload a file before the Retailer could accept and move to fulfillment; now one is auto-generated the instant a `Deal` is created (`Deal.invoiceAutoGenerated`), rendered at `/deal/[id]/invoice`, so the Retailer is never blocked. The seller can still upload their own file to override it — same anonymization rule as everywhere else: each party's own side is real, the counterparty is only ever shown as their `anonHandle` (Broker/Admin see both real). **Real accounting-platform integration is intentionally not built yet** — the human has a separate, more mature project (`ai-ledger`, an AI accounting/tax platform with a real `Invoice`/`Customer`/`ApiKey` data model) they want this to eventually hand off to for real payment processing via a cannabis-friendly bank. That integration is deferred until (a) `ai-ledger`'s own concurrent-session lock clears — see its CLAUDE.md §9, two sessions collided there once already — and (b) its actual invoice-creation API surface is understood. The in-app auto-generated invoice is meant to be the always-working default; the `ai-ledger` connection becomes a selectable option layered on top, not a replacement.
- **Toll-processing / split contracts** (`lib/split-contracts.ts`) — a Processor can source a Grower's raw listing not for a flat price, but for a % share of the finished product's eventual value (`SplitContract` model). Flow: Processor browses `/processor/sourcing` (active Grower listings only) and proposes a split; Grower accepts/rejects from their listing detail page (same anonymized-handle pattern as regular offers); once accepted the listing closes like a normal `Deal` would; Processor logs a yield once processing is done, then either party keys in a settlement value and the split is computed — **the platform takes no cut of this**, unlike §11's `Deal` commission below. Deliberately simpler than the price/terms negotiation state machine: single propose → accept/reject, no counter-offer loop. Physical pickup/transport for a settled contract is coordinated directly between Grower and Processor for now — it is **not** wired into the `Shipment`/Transporter flow yet, which means (like the rest of this app, see §2) it produces no METRC-equivalent paper trail. That's a deferred follow-up, not a solved problem — add it to §8 if picked up.
- **Product acceptance after delivery** (`lib/commission.ts`'s `acceptProduct`/`rejectProduct`) — once a `Shipment` reaches `delivered`, the Retailer gets an explicit accept/reject step (`Deal.productStatus`). Accepting shows a "this transaction is final, no in-app dispute process" notice before it's confirmed, and is the trigger that computes any commission amount owed (§11). Rejecting is only *recorded* — the app does not run a refund/return/dispute workflow; that's real-world, off-platform, same caveat as §2's transfer language.

## 11. Broker commission (added 2026-08-16 — reverses §9's original no-commission stance)

**This was a confirmed, explicit reversal, not a silent decision.** The human was shown the exact tradeoff — commission is the pattern that makes the platform look like a broker taking a cut of a cannabis transfer, sharpening §2's still-open "is broker a recognized MI license category" question rather than staying clear of it — and chose to proceed anyway. If a future session is asked to touch commission logic and something about it seems inconsistent with the blind-marketplace/no-transfer posture elsewhere in this doc, that's expected: this section is a deliberate carve-out, not an oversight to "fix" back to the original stance.

- **Any broker can set commission terms on any `Deal`** (`lib/commission.ts`'s `setCommission`) — same platform-wide visibility as everything else a broker sees (decision #2). Terms: a **rate from 0-100%, entirely the broker's own judgment call per deal** — there is no platform-default or preset band; the only ceiling is the mathematical one (can't take more than the deal is worth). Payer is also broker-chosen: grower pays, retailer pays, or split by a broker-chosen grower/retailer percentage.
- **The dollar amount is computed once, at product acceptance** (§10's `acceptProduct`), not before — a broker can freely edit rate/payer terms any time before that point without anything downstream depending on the old number. After computation, editing terms does *not* silently recalculate — see the comment in `setCommission`.
- **Tracked, not processed.** `Commission.status` goes `pending` → `paid` via a manual broker (or Admin) action (`markCommissionPaid`) — this app never moves money itself, same as every other dollar figure here (§8). No card processing, no ACH, nothing programmatic.
- Real payment rails (the human's separate `ai-ledger` project, connected to a cannabis-friendly bank) are the eventual path for actually *collecting* commission — same deferred-integration status as §10's invoice section.

## 12. License registry auto-populate + auto-approval, and a relaxed posting gate (added 2026-08-16)

Two related changes, both requested by the human directly:

- **The state license number lookup is real now, not just admin-manual review.** `LicenseRegistry` (`web/prisma/schema.prisma`) holds ~3,300 rows imported from the state of Michigan's own CRA license exports (5 files: Class B Grower, Class C Grower, Processor, Retailer, Secure Transporter — no Broker category exists in the state data, which is exactly §2's still-open question, not resolved here) via `scripts/parse-license-csvs.mjs` + `scripts/import-license-registry.mjs`. At signup, entering a license number triggers `lib/license-registry.ts`'s `lookupLicense` (exposed to the client via `/api/license-lookup` for live autofill, and re-run independently server-side in `signup/actions.ts` — the client's autofill is never trusted for the actual decision) which auto-fills business name and address, and — **only if the state's own status for that record is exactly `"Active"`** — sets `licenseVerification: "approved"` immediately instead of leaving it `"unverified"` for the Admin queue. Anything else (no match, `"License Void"`, `"Closed - Suspended"`, `"Revoked"`) falls back to the existing manual Admin review, unchanged.
- **This is tracked as a distinct thing from a real Admin approval, on purpose.** `User.licenseAutoMatched` is `true` only when the license was auto-approved this way. The honest caveat, stated plainly rather than glossed over: matching a license *number* is not proof that the person typing it in actually owns or represents that license — MI license numbers are largely public record. Auto-matched accounts show a distinct "auto-matched" badge in Admin's user list (`/admin`) precisely so a human retains an audit trail and can revoke one if something looks off, even though it wasn't blocking anyone up front. Don't quietly turn this into "auto-matched == verified" anywhere in the UI — the badge exists so nobody makes that mistake.
- **Phone-number enrichment from the platform owner's own lead list — done.** Once the Lead Directory (MI) list was available as a real CSV export (see §20's `Lead` import), `scripts/enrich-license-registry-phone.mjs` ran a business-name-matching merge pass: normalizes both sides' names (strips punctuation and entity suffixes like LLC/Inc), builds a name→phone map from the 530-row CRM export, and fills `LicenseRegistry.phone` only where it was still null and only for `grower_b`/`grower_c` rows (the Lead Directory list is specifically the platform owner's grower-solicitation list, so matching against processor/retailer/transporter rows would risk false-positive name collisions across unrelated business types). Matched and filled **581 of 1,542 phone-less grower rows**. Best-effort, not a guarantee — a name match isn't proof the phone is current or even the right branch of a multi-location business. **Not yet surfaced anywhere in the UI** — `lib/license-registry.ts`'s `lookupLicense` (used at signup) doesn't select or return `phone` yet; wiring it into signup autofill is a natural follow-up but wasn't part of what was asked here.
- **Monthly refresh has now actually been run once**, via `scripts/refresh-license-registry.mjs` (a standalone-script twin of `lib/license-registry-import.ts`'s `importLicenseRegistryCsv`, needed because `tsx` can't resolve the `"server-only"` import outside the Next.js build) — same 5 state files, category auto-detected per row from the state's own "Record Type" text so files can be passed in any order. Row count held steady at 3,302 (same underlying dataset upserted in place, not a duplicate load). This was run from the CLI directly, not through the `/admin/data-uploads` UI — the UI path exercises the same underlying logic but wasn't itself clicked through this time.
- **The listing/offer posting gate was relaxed** (`lib/seller-actions.ts`, `lib/retailer-actions.ts`, `lib/processor-actions.ts`) — previously `licenseVerification !== "approved"` blocked posting/offering entirely while pending review; now only `licenseVerification === "rejected"` blocks it, so a normal `"unverified"` (pending Admin, or pending a registry match that didn't auto-approve) account can still post listings and make/respond to offers immediately. **This was not extended to transporter shipment assignment** (`lib/shipments.ts` still requires `"approved"` to be selected as a deal's transporter or show up as the default) — that's a physical-dispatch decision, not what was asked to loosen, and it's a different risk tier than posting inventory or negotiating a price.

## 13. Sales Rep role, shipment scheduling, freshness nudges, and a real post-rejection workflow (added 2026-08-16)

A batch of workflow requests, walked through and implemented together:

- **New role: Sales Rep** (`sales_rep`, `lib/sales-actions.ts`). Same trust tier as Broker — sees real Grower/Processor identity, not a licensed cultivator themselves (not in `LICENSED_ROLES`/`ADDRESS_ROLES`). Solicits an inventory list from a grower/processor off-platform, then builds it into a listing at `/sales/listings/new` using a seller search (`SellerPicker` component, `searchAssistableSellers`) — the listing posts under the **chosen seller's own identity** (`Listing.postedById`), with `Listing.createdBySalesRepId` as a pure audit trail, never shown to buyers. **Admin got the identical capability** (`/admin/listings/new`, same underlying `handleCreateListingAsAssistant`) per an explicit follow-up ask — one shared function, two entry points, gated separately by `requireRole("sales_rep")` / `requireRole("admin")` in each page's own search-action file.
- **Growers can still post their own menus directly, unchanged** — Sales Rep/Admin assistance is additive, not a replacement path.
- **Shipment scheduling** (`Shipment.scheduledPickupAt/scheduledDeliveryAt/growerAcceptedSchedule/retailerAcceptedSchedule`, `lib/shipments.ts`'s `proposeShipmentSchedule`/`acceptShipmentSchedule`). The transporter proposes a pickup/delivery window from their shipment detail page; the grower and retailer each accept it separately from their own deal panel (`ScheduleAcceptPanel`). `advanceShipmentStatus` now **blocks the assigned → picked_up transition** until both have accepted, but only if a schedule was ever proposed — deals that skip scheduling entirely aren't gated, so this stays backward compatible. Re-proposing a new time resets both acceptance flags.
- **Retailers get a "Not interested" dismiss button** (`ListingDismissal` model, `lib/dismissals.ts`, `DismissButton` component) alongside Watchlist — hides a listing from that retailer's own feed only, reversible, never affects what anyone else sees. This is a per-viewer feed filter, not a change to the listing itself, so it doesn't touch decision #3's bulletin-board rule.
- **Listing freshness nudges** (`Listing.lastConfirmedAt`, `lib/listings.ts`'s `confirmListingFresh`). Sellers post multiple times a day and stale listings degrade the feed, so: the retailer feed flags anything not confirmed available in 3+ days ("Not confirmed in Nd"), offers a "Recently confirmed available" sort, and the seller's own dashboard/listing-detail page shows the same staleness plus a one-click "Still available? Confirm" button that bumps `lastConfirmedAt`. **No actual push/email reminder** — there's no background-job infra in this app (same gap as §8's deferred SMS/push item), so this is informational-on-view only, not a proactive nudge sent to anyone.
- **Post-rejection now has a real in-app workflow**, not just a recorded dead-end (`ProductRejection` model, `lib/commission.ts`'s `chooseReturn`/`proposeRejectionCounter`/`acceptRejectionCounter`/`requireReturnInsteadOfCounter`). Rejecting delivery now **requires a reason**. The retailer then picks one of two paths: send the product back outright, or propose a revised price reflecting its condition. The grower either accepts the revised price (the deal closes at the new `finalPrice`) or insists on the return. Single round, no back-and-forth counter loop — same "deliberately simpler" scope cut as `SplitContract`. **This still is NOT a refund/payment/return-shipping engine** — the resolution is tracked, the actual physical return and any money movement stay off-platform, same caveat as §2/§8 everywhere else in this doc. Don't let "there's now a workflow" get read as "this app handles returns."

## 14. Negotiated rejection fee (added 2026-08-16)

Distinct from §11's broker commission on purpose: this is a term the **buyer and seller negotiate themselves**, during their own offer/counter rounds (`OfferRound.rejectionFeeRate`/`rejectionFeePayer`) — not something a broker sets after the fact. Confirmed with the human: the fee is a % of deal value, freely negotiated like price (no platform-wide default rate), while the *payer* defaults to a 50/50 split if the parties never discuss it (either side can propose grower-pays/retailer-pays/split instead, same as they'd counter on price). Resolved onto `Deal.rejectionFeeRate`/`rejectionFeePayer` at acceptance the same way `finalPrice` is. The actual dollar amount is computed at rejection time (`lib/commission.ts`'s `rejectProduct`, stored on `ProductRejection.feeAmount`/`feeGrowerOwes`/`feeRetailerOwes`) since that's the event that triggers it — a rate of 0 (never negotiated) means no fee at all. Tracked via `feeStatus`/`markRejectionFeePaid` (broker or admin marks it settled), same "this app never moves money" posture as Commission (§8/§11) — no card processing, no ACH.

## 15. Cross-role ratings (added 2026-08-16)

Extends the existing seller rating (§10's `sellerRating`) to every role, same honesty rule throughout: **no fabricated default for someone with no history yet** — a user with zero relevant deals/shipments shows no score, not a made-up middling one.

- **Retailer rating** (`lib/market.ts`'s `retailerRating`) — this app doesn't process real payment (§8), so "pays on time" is proxied by how fast a retailer accepts the invoice once it's on file (the fastest real signal actually available), combined with how often their deals end in a rejected delivery. Surfaced to the seller, per-thread, via `retailerRatingForThread` — resolves through a `threadId` so the seller-side caller never has to be handed `retailerId` directly, same anonymization-boundary pattern as `sellerRatingForListing`.
- **Transporter rating** (`transporterRating`) — % of shipments delivered on/before their scheduled window (§13), among shipments that actually had one proposed; falls back to plain delivery-completion rate when no schedule was ever used, so adopting §13's scheduling feature isn't a prerequisite for having a score at all.

## 21. Pre-launch security review, prompted by a deployment ask (added 2026-08-17)

The human asked to deploy to Vercel to get a real shareable link. Before doing that, a security audit was run against the whole codebase (not a diff — there was no git remote configured to diff against, and the ask was "is this safe to put on the internet," which a diff-based review wouldn't answer). Two real, exploitable issues came back; both are fixed. A separate, more consequential gap was also found by hand while answering the human's own question about data exposure — that one required a human decision, not just a fix, and got one.

**A. RSC payload leak of the counterparty's real internal user ID (fixed).** `lib/offers.ts`'s `threadForRetailerListing` queried `deal: { include: DEAL_SHIPMENT_INCLUDE }` with no `select` — Prisma's `include` without `select` returns every scalar field on `Deal`, including `sellerId` (the real Grower/Processor's internal `User.id`). That object flowed straight into `<DealPanelRetailer>`, a `"use client"` component — and **every prop passed into a Client Component is serialized into the RSC/flight payload sent to the browser**, regardless of what the component's own TypeScript type or JSX actually reads. The component's local `Deal` type omitted `sellerId`, which made it look safe in review; it did nothing at runtime. A Retailer viewing their own accepted deal could find the Grower/Processor's real internal ID sitting in the page source. This is exactly what CLAUDE.md §3/§6 already warn about — "the UI hiding a field is not enough if the API response includes it" — the RSC payload *is* an API response here. Fixed by switching `threadForRetailerListing` to an explicit `select` naming only the fields `DealPanelRetailer` actually needs. `threadsForListing` (the seller-side equivalent) got the same narrowing for defense in depth, even though `DealPanelSeller` is currently server-only and not vulnerable today — relying on a component staying server-only forever is fragile.

**B. Unauthenticated, unrate-limited license-registry scraping endpoint (fixed, partially).** `/api/license-lookup` takes no auth by design — it's called from the signup form before an account exists, so there's no session available to require. That makes it the one place in the app an anonymous caller can hit at all, and an anonymous script could iterate license-number formats through it to re-extract the full ~3,300-row `LicenseRegistry` (including `hasDisciplinaryAction`). Added a per-IP in-memory sliding-window limiter (20 requests/minute) as a stopgap. **Known limitation, stated plainly rather than glossed over:** this is in-memory, so it only throttles a single server instance and resets on cold start — it will not hold once this runs on more than one serverless instance, which Vercel routinely does. A real fix needs a shared store (Upstash Redis or similar) keyed by IP; not added here since that's a new external dependency the human hasn't asked for.

**C. Broker and Sales Rep self-signup granted instant, zero-review, platform-wide access (fixed — human confirmed the fix direction).** This wasn't found by the audit — it surfaced when the human asked "what does going live mean, do we add data at that time, we don't want it exposed to the public," and answering that honestly required checking who could actually reach the data once the app is public. `signup/actions.ts`'s `SIGNUP_ROLES` let anyone self-signup as `broker` or `sales_rep`; neither is in `LICENSED_ROLES`, so `licenseVerification` was set straight to `"approved"` with no review step at all (unlike Grower/Processor/Retailer/Transporter, which at least get the manual Admin queue when there's no clean registry match). Concretely: before this fix, a stranger visiting the public site could sign up as Sales Rep and immediately see the entire Marketing Suite — all 2,243 real business leads with phone numbers — or sign up as Broker and see every real negotiation on the platform, in real identity, on both sides. No waiting, no approval, nothing. This directly contradicts how both roles are already described elsewhere in this document (§13, §20) as "the marketplace operator's own staff," not third parties. **Flagged to the human rather than fixed silently**, per this document's own standing "flag, don't decide" posture for anything touching who can see real identity (§9) — asked which of three fixes to apply (Admin-only creation / keep public but gate on Admin approval / leave as-is), and the human chose Admin-only creation. Implemented as: `broker`/`sales_rep` removed from `SIGNUP_ROLES` (`signup/actions.ts`, enforced server-side — not just removed from the dropdown, since a client-only restriction is trivial to bypass by posting the form directly) and from `signup-form.tsx`'s `ROLE_OPTIONS`; `lib/admin.ts`'s new `createStaffAccount` plus a new `/admin/staff/new` page let an Admin create either account type directly instead.

**Also fixed while in there:** `npm audit` turned up one high-severity transitive advisory (`nanoid < 3.3.18`, pulled in via `postcss`/Tailwind's build tooling, not attacker-reachable at runtime) — `npm audit fix` cleared it with no breaking changes; `npm audit` now reports zero vulnerabilities.

**Confirmed as already-fine, not new findings:** authorization on every server action (every `*-actions.ts` function checked calls `requireRole()` and verifies the caller owns the specific record being mutated, not just "is logged in as the right role" — no IDOR found); the read-side anonymization boundary in `lib/listings.ts`/`lib/offers.ts` beyond finding A above; auth config (`bcrypt`, JWT sessions, no hardcoded secrets, `.env` correctly gitignored); no `$queryRaw`/`$executeRaw` usage anywhere. The already-documented deferred items (local disk storage, unencrypted METRC/POS credentials, no rate limiting anywhere else, no live METRC calls — §8/§18/§19) were confirmed still accurate, not re-flagged as new problems.

**Still true after all of this: this app has not been reviewed by a Michigan cannabis attorney (§2), and that has nothing to do with any of the above.** Fixing these three issues makes the *software* safer to expose publicly; it does not touch the still-open legal question of whether the Broker role needs its own license category, or make real METRC/payment integration any less blocked on that review. Don't let "the security audit passed" read as "this is cleared for real product."

- **Cash drawer / receipt printer hardware is flagged, not built.** A browser page can't trigger a cash drawer directly — that goes through the receipt printer's kick-out port via a printer-specific protocol (ESC/POS over network, or a vendor SDK like Star CloudPRNT/Epson ePOS-Print). - **METRC package tag is a real, stated gap, not glossed over.** METRC sales-receipt submission requires a package tag METRC already knows about, and nothing upstream in this app (Listing/Deal) captures one — Growers/Processors don't record METRC tags anywhere here. So a Retailer must key the real tag in by hand at intake for `submitSaleToMetrc` to attempt a live call; without one, the sale still completes and is tracked in-app, `Sale.metrcStatus` is explicitly `"skipped_no_tag"` — never faked as submitted.
- **`MetrcVendorConfig.useSandbox`** (default `true`, Admin toggle at `/admin/metrc`) — since this is the app's first live external METRC call, it defaults to MI's sandbox endpoint (`sandbox-api-mi.metrc.com`) rather than production (`api-mi.metrc.com`) until Admin has verified a real submission end-to-end. `/admin/metrc` also now shows a live feed of recent `Sale.metrcStatus` outcomes across every retailer, for compliance oversight.
- **Credentials are now encrypted at rest** (`lib/crypto.ts`, AES-256-GCM, key derived via HKDF from the already-required `NEXTAUTH_SECRET` — no new secret to provision). `MetrcConnection.userApiKey` and `MetrcVendorConfig.vendorApiKey` were previously plaintext with an explicit "not encrypted yet" note, acceptable while nothing used them for a real call — that stopped being true the moment `submitSaleToMetrc` exists. Settings forms no longer pre-fill the actual/masked key into an editable input (a mask shown for reference, blank input means "keep what's on file"); `PosConnection.apiKey` (the unrelated Dutchie/Treez/Flowhub/Cova stub scaffolding) is untouched — still stub-only.
- **Tax is explicit and editable, not a certified engine.** MI adult-use cannabis retail carries 6% sales tax + 10% marijuana excise tax; the register defaults `taxRatePercent` to 16% but it's a plain editable number stamped onto each `Sale`, not a real tax-rate lookup. Stated here rather than left implicit, same honesty convention as the seller-rating "no fabricated default" rule (§10).
- **Not built / caveat on what "live" means here:** there are no real MI METRC credentials in this dev environment, so `submitSaleToMetrc` is built to METRC's documented Sales Receipts API contract but has **not** been exercised against a live sandbox — what's verified locally is the request shape, the `skipped_no_tag` short-circuit, and that a network/API failure is caught and recorded on the `Sale` rather than crashing the register. A real sandbox account is needed before flipping any retailer's connection to production.

## 25. Customer order-ahead / pickup (added 2026-08-17)

Three scope calls, made explicitly and recorded here, each avoiding a materially bigger/riskier version of what was asked:

- **Per-retailer storefront (`/order/<slug>`), not a platform-wide marketplace.** No cross-retailer browsing. Turning this into a Weedmaps-style aggregator this platform itself runs would be a much larger compliance surface (facilitating retail sales across many licensees to the public) than giving each existing retailer their own ordering tool for their own operation.
- **Guest checkout, pay at pickup — no accounts, no online payment.** `Order.customerName`/`customerPhone` are free text, same "not an account, never used to re-identify" posture as `Sale.customerName` (§24). Money changes hands in person, tender selected at fulfillment the same way it already happens at the register. - **Self-attestation age checkbox (`Order.ageAttested`), not real ID verification.** ID-scanning hardware is still its own unbuilt, separately-flagged item (§24). Real age verification happens the normal way: in person, at pickup.

**Architecture — `Order` is deliberately not a `Sale`.** `lib/pos.ts`'s `createSale` decrements real, METRC-tracked inventory the instant it runs — correct for an in-store register ring-up, wrong for a stranger submitting a public web form (a no-show, a stock race with a walk-in sale, or someone attesting falsely shouldn't falsely deplete real inventory or trigger a real METRC event). `Order`/`OrderLineItem` are a new pre-sale record; `lib/pos.ts`'s `fulfillOrder` is the only place an `Order` becomes a real `Sale` — it re-validates current stock (which may have moved since the order was placed) and then calls the **existing** `createSale(...)` as-is, reusing its transaction/decrement/METRC-submit logic rather than duplicating it. `Order` never touches inventory or METRC before that point.

**Why the public menu never joins back to the wholesale `Listing`/`ListingMedia`.** `lib/storefront.ts`'s `publicMenuForRetailer` uses a narrow `select` on `InventoryLot` only — never `include`, never `InventoryLot.dealId → Deal → Listing`. `Deal.sellerId` is exactly the field this document's own §21 audit log documents as a real, already-fixed RSC-payload leak of a Grower/Processor's real internal ID; reusing that join here would risk the identical mistake shipped to an anonymous public visitor instead of a logged-in retailer. `ListingMedia` photos are also only best-effort redacted (§18) — an accepted risk *within* the existing platform trust boundary, not one that should be multiplied out to the general public. `InventoryLot` has no photo field and this feature doesn't add one — menu cards use the same `Leaf`-icon placeholder `components/retailer/listing-card.tsx` already uses for a listing with no photo.

**Known, stated gap: no enforcement of Michigan's per-transaction possession/purchase limits.** Not silently skipped — flagged here, same honesty convention as §24's tax-rate caveat.

**What shipped:** `User.storefrontSlug` (retailer-set in Settings, URL-safe, distinct in purpose from `anonHandle` — that field anonymizes identity across the wholesale blind-marketplace boundary, this is the opposite, a retailer's own real identity meant to be shared publicly); `Order`/`OrderLineItem` models; `lib/storefront.ts` (public, no `requireRole` anywhere in it — the one file in this app with no session to check); the order-placement server action rate-limited with the exact same in-memory per-IP sliding-window pattern as `/api/license-lookup`; a notification to the retailer via the existing `lib/notifications.ts` (no new mechanism); a fifth **Orders** tab in the existing `/retailer/pos` `PosTabs` (Mark ready / Fulfill / Cancel) rather than a new top-level nav item, since it's register-adjacent work.

**Incident during this build, unrelated to the feature itself, recorded for the record:** while generating this migration, a `prisma migrate diff --shadow-database-url` command was mistakenly pointed at `DIRECT_URL` — which turned out to be the *same* Supabase database as `DATABASE_URL`, just a different connection port (pooled vs. direct), not a genuinely separate disposable database. Prisma's shadow-database process reset it, wiping all data in the live database (schema/tables were unaffected — only rows). Recovered via Supabase's daily scheduled backup (the most recent available; point-in-time recovery wasn't enabled), restored in place, then `prisma migrate deploy` re-applied every migration from that backup's point forward — including reordering this feature's own migration folder, which had an earlier timestamp than the POS tables (`sales`/`inventory_lots`) it depends on and so failed to apply in the wrong sequence on the first replay (Postgres rolled that back cleanly inside a transaction, no partial state). Standing rule going forward: never point any "shadow database" parameter at anything without first explicitly confirming it's a genuinely separate, empty, disposable database — for additive schema changes going forward, hand-write the migration SQL directly from the schema diff instead of using any tool that needs a live shadow database at all.

## 30. Commercial-grade, multi-location POS hardening (added 2026-08-17)

The human asked for the Retailer POS to genuinely handle heavy transaction volume with multiple registers running simultaneously across multiple store locations — not just work correctly for one quiet register. Reviewing that question surfaced a real, confirmed bug before any of the hardening work started: `createSale`'s inventory decrement wasn't concurrency-safe and could oversell stock when two terminals raced for the same lot. Three scope decisions were confirmed via AskUserQuestion before planning: **scale target is multiple store locations** (not just one busy shop), **code-only, no new vendors** (everything below uses the existing stack — Next.js/Prisma/Postgres via Supabase — or open-source local tooling, never Sentry/Upstash/a hosted queue), and **real METRC credentials aren't available yet**, so live-fire sandbox testing stays out of scope here.

**A. Inventory overselling, fixed.** `createSale` (`lib/pos.ts`) used to read `quantityRemaining`, check it in application code, then write back a value computed from that stale read — no atomic guard at write time. Fixed with Prisma's `updateMany` combined with `{ decrement: qty }` and a `WHERE quantityRemaining >= qty` guard, checking `result.count === 0` to detect a lost race and throw a clean "sold out" error instead of silently overselling. A second guarded `updateMany` flips status to `"depleted"`, idempotent regardless of how many concurrent sales raced to zero a lot out.

**B. Sale-numbering race, fixed.** The prior `SELECT MAX(saleNumber) + 1` then `INSERT` pattern threw a raw, unhandled `P2002` under real concurrent checkouts at the same retailer — caught only by the `@@unique([retailerId, saleNumber])` constraint already on `Sale`, which rejected the second insert rather than handling it gracefully. Replaced with a new `SaleCounter` model (`retailerId @id, value Int`), incremented atomically via `upsert` + `{ increment: 1 }` inside the same transaction — Postgres's row-level lock on that single row correctly serializes concurrent terminals at one retailer/location without any cross-location contention, since each location's counter is an independent row. Migration hand-written per the standing rule from the §25 incident (`prisma/migrations/20260817070000_sale_counter/`, applied via `migrate deploy`, no shadow-database tooling touched).

**C. `createInventoryLotFromDeal`'s unique-constraint race.** `InventoryLot.dealId` was already `@unique`, so a genuine double-intake race was already rejected at the DB level — the code just didn't catch that `P2002` gracefully. Now matches `setStorefrontSlug`'s existing friendly-catch convention instead of leaking a raw Prisma error.

**Transaction timeouts, widened.** Fixing (A) surfaced a second real issue during testing: Prisma's default interactive-transaction timeouts (`maxWait` 2s, `timeout` 5s) aren't generous enough once transactions correctly start queuing behind each other for a hot row's lock — under 12-way concurrent contention on a single lot, later transactions in the queue hit a raw "transaction not found" error instead of either succeeding or failing cleanly. `createSale`'s `$transaction` call now passes `{ maxWait: 10_000, timeout: 10_000 }` — that queuing is (A)'s fix working as intended, not a bug, so the timeout needs enough headroom to let it resolve to a clean outcome either way.

**Cross-terminal sync (Phase 1).** A terminal used to only learn about another terminal's sale on its next manual navigation — harmless after (A)'s fix (a stale read just causes a clean retry), but slow. New `components/retailer/pos/live-refresh.tsx` polls `router.refresh()` every few seconds while `/retailer/pos` is visible (paused via the Page Visibility API when the tab isn't focused), refreshing server-fetched props for Inventory/Orders/Intake without disturbing `RegisterPanel`'s own client-side cart state. Supabase Realtime (a feature of the Postgres project already in use, not a new vendor) is the noted escalation path if polling latency ever proves insufficient at real scale — not built now, since polling should be tried against real usage first.

**METRC resilience (Phase 2A).** `submitSaleToMetrc` (`lib/metrc-integration.ts`) now retries with bounded jittered exponential backoff (`postWithRetry`, up to 4 attempts) on a 429 or 5xx — MI METRC enforces a per-license rate limit, and near-simultaneous sales under real multi-terminal load can trigger one. A 4xx other than 429 (bad credentials, malformed request) isn't retried, since retrying an inherently-invalid request just wastes time. The call is also moved off the checkout critical path via Next's `after()` — a register no longer sits waiting on METRC's latency before the next customer can be rung up; the `Sale.metrcStatus` row is still the durable record, checked later at `/admin/metrc`.

**Postgres-backed rate limiting (Phase 2B).** The two existing in-memory per-IP limiters (`/api/license-lookup`, the storefront order action — both flagged in the §21 security audit as not holding across more than one serverless instance) are replaced by a shared `lib/rate-limit.ts` backed by a new `RateLimitBucket` table (`key @id, windowStart, count`), an atomic fixed-window upsert via one `$queryRaw` `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT` statement. Fixed-window, not a precise sliding log — a request right at a window boundary could in theory see slightly more than the limit across two adjacent windows, an acceptable tradeoff for blunting scripted abuse (not enforcing an exact quota) at the cost of one cheap upsert per request rather than a row per hit. This directly resolves the exact "won't hold once this runs on more than one instance" gap §21 already flagged, and is squarely what "multiple instances running simultaneously" requires.

**Automated tests, added — none existed before this.** `vitest` (dev dependency only, `npm test` / `npm run test:load`), with a `server-only` alias shim and a `next/server` `after()` shim in `vitest.setup.ts` (both packages/APIs only resolve inside Next's own bundler/request-scope, not under plain Vitest — documented inline in each shim). Pure THC/holiday-watermark calculation logic was extracted out of `register-panel.tsx` (a `"use client"` component whose other imports drag in the full NextAuth chain, unsafe to import from a test runner) into a new dependency-free `lib/pos-calculations.ts`, re-exported from the component with no behavior change. Coverage: money math (subtotal/tax/total) via real `createSale` calls, the THC/holiday pure functions (formalizing the ad hoc 13-case validation script mentioned in §24, now a real regression test), and — the actual point of this whole effort — a concurrency test firing a dozen parallel `createSale` calls at a lot with quantity intentionally exceeding stock, asserting `quantityRemaining` never goes negative, exactly the right count succeed, and every rejection is one of the two clean expected messages rather than a raw DB error. All against the real dev database (Postgres's own row-locking behavior is what's under test — a mock can't meaningfully stand in for that).

**Local load test (Phase 4), no k6 install needed.** `k6` itself is a system binary this environment can't install (no package manager available here) — `src/lib/pos.load.test.ts` (run via `npm run test:load`, its own `vitest.load.config.ts` so it's excluded from the regular `npm test` pass) simulates 3 locations × 4 terminals × 15 checkout attempts each against a deliberately small, shared 5-lot catalog per location. Real result against the dev database: 161/180 succeeded, 19 failed with the expected clean messages, **zero unexpected failures, zero oversells, zero duplicate sale numbers.** Also surfaced an honest tradeoff worth stating plainly: checkout latency climbed to ~3.8s median under this deliberately extreme contention (many terminals hammering only a handful of shared SKUs) — the cost of correctly serializing writes on a hot row rather than an unbounded scaling ceiling. Real floor traffic spread across a full catalog won't see this; it only shows up when many terminals genuinely fight over the exact same few products at once.

**Admin-wide system health view (Phase 5).** New `/admin/system-health` (`lib/admin.ts`'s `systemHealthSnapshot`) — recently voided sales and currently-rate-limited identifiers, across every retailer/location at once, built entirely from data already captured elsewhere (no new logging infrastructure). Recent METRC submission outcomes keep their own dedicated page at `/admin/metrc`, not duplicated here. The rate-limit section is a **live snapshot of the current window, not a history** — `RateLimitBucket` only tracks each identifier's current count, not a log of past rejection events, so showing "currently over the limit" is the honest thing to surface rather than fabricating a timeline that isn't actually captured. Same honesty note as elsewhere in this app: this is a dashboard someone checks, not proactive alerting — no SMS/email/paging exists anywhere yet.

**An interactive credentials checklist was built and handed to the human** (not part of the codebase) enumerating what's needed outside the repo: real METRC per-licensee keys and the platform vendor key (required, not yet obtained), a Vercel account and git remote (needed before deploying — still not done, a pre-existing loose end), a Supabase plan-tier check for multi-location volume, and cash-drawer/receipt-printer hardware (still blocked on the human choosing a vendor, per §23).

**Verified:** `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npx next build` clean after every phase. `npm test` — 18/18 passing (money math, THC/holiday pure functions, both concurrency tests). `npm run test:load` — 1/1 passing with the real-run numbers above. Live in the actual running app: scanned the seeded Blue Dream lot (9 lb on hand) at `/retailer/pos`, forced the cart quantity to 999, and hit Complete sale — the register displayed `"Only 9 lb of Blue Dream left."` cleanly, styled consistently with the rest of the UI, no crash and no raw error. Confirmed afterward against the database that the rejected attempt left `quantityRemaining` untouched at 9 and created no phantom `Sale` row — the guard rejects before any write happens. This exercises the exact user-facing path a real register hits when it loses a stock race, on top of the automated concurrency test that already proves the underlying atomic guard holds under real parallel load.

## 31. Register customer panel — identity, purchase habits, discounts, loyalty, purchase limits (added 2026-08-18)

The human shared a Flowhub cashier screenshot and asked whether Xcelerate POS should look similar. The real answer, worked through together: don't copy Flowhub's specific visual brand (Xcelerate keeps its own gold/amber identity, now built from the real logo), but the density gap on that screen — customer purchase habits, loyalty points, discounts, a purchase-limit tracker — is a **feature** gap, not a styling one. A full Flowhub feature audit was built as an interactive artifact and handed to the human (filterable Have/Partial/Don't-have, grouped by category) before any code changed, so the scope decision was made with real information rather than a guess.

**The one real architectural decision, confirmed via AskUserQuestion before writing any code.** All four of those features need the register to recognize the same customer across visits. `Sale.customerName`/`Order.customerName`/`customerPhone` had always been documented as "receipt-only, never used to look up or re-identify" — a deliberate no-PII stance from earlier in the build (§24's loyalty-system decline explicitly cited "no `Customer` model, no points ledger, no accounts, no PII retention beyond the receipt"). The human confirmed reversing this specifically: add a lightweight `Customer` record — name + phone as the lookup key, no password, no online login, nothing beyond what a budtender types in to recognize a repeat visitor. Scoped per retailer like every other retailer-owned record in this app; no cross-retailer customer sharing, no platform-wide customer graph.

**Separate from, and explicitly not a walk-back of, an unrelated request declined the same day.** The human separately asked for an admin "backdoor" to split a 10 lb sale into fake 2.5 oz visits spread across several days, to disguise it as compliant under Michigan's per-visit purchase limit. That was refused outright — it's a records-falsification mechanism, not a compliance feature, and would push fabricated data into METRC. The purchase-limit tracking built here is the opposite: it makes a real cumulative total *visible and enforceable*, nothing about it helps evade the limit it displays.

**Data model** (`web/prisma/schema.prisma`, hand-written migration + a same-day backfill migration — see below): `Customer` (`retailerId`, `name`, `phone` normalized as the `@@unique([retailerId, phone])` lookup key, `notes`, `loyaltyPointsBalance` — a cached running total kept correct by `createSale`'s own atomically guarded increment, same discipline as `InventoryLot.quantityRemaining`'s decrement). `LoyaltyLedgerEntry` (every accrual and redemption logged as a real row — `points`, `reason`, optional `saleId` — same "log every event" convention as `SalesRepCommission`/`MarketingMessage`/`ShipmentEvent`, not just an opaque balance mutation). `Sale.customerId` (new, nullable — additive; `Sale.customerName` is untouched, still the receipt-time snapshot, so a customer's name changing later never rewrites a past receipt). `SaleLineItem.discountAmount` (flat $, register-entered — `lineTotal` is still the real net charged amount; this field only exists so the receipt/register can show the struck-through original price). `User.loyaltyPointsPerDollar`/`dailyPurchaseLimitOz` (both plain editable numbers, not certified figures — same posture as the existing tax-rate field — set on `/retailer/settings`).

**Purchase-limit tracking is scoped honestly, not asserted as a certified compliance check** (`lib/customers.ts`'s `todaysPurchaseTotals`). Michigan's limit is expressed in flower-equivalent ounces, and the equivalency ratio between flower and concentrate/vape/edible products is real regulatory judgment this build should not guess at — getting it wrong in a compliance-adjacent feature is worse than not building it. So: weight-sold (`unit === "lb"`) line items convert to ounces with confidence (unambiguous unit math, no legal judgment involved) and sum into the bar shown on the customer panel; unit/liter-sold lines (concentrate, vape, edible) are tracked as their own separate running count, explicitly labeled as *not* converted into the flower-equivalent figure — same honest-gap pattern already used by the existing THC-equivalency calculator's liquid-concentrate exclusion. The bar and the day's total only appear once a `Customer` is attached to the sale, since cross-visit tracking is exactly what the identity decision above enables — a walk-in shows no bar, not a fabricated one.

**`lib/customers.ts`** (new): `findOrCreateCustomer` (normalizes phone, upserts, friendly-catches the `P2002` race the same way `setStorefrontSlug` already does for its own unique constraint), `lookupCustomerByPhone`, `customerPurchaseHabits` (avg spend, last-sale date, sales/month, top categories by $ — returns `null` for zero history rather than a fabricated default, same convention as `lib/market.ts`'s seller/retailer ratings), `todaysPurchaseTotals`, `redeemLoyaltyPoints` (atomically guarded decrement, same pattern as the inventory fix — validates balance and rejects cleanly rather than allowing it to go negative).

**`createSale` extended** (`web/src/lib/pos.ts`) — accepts optional `customerId` and per-line `discountAmount`; within the same transaction that already does the atomically-guarded stock decrement, also does a plain `increment` on `Customer.loyaltyPointsBalance` (a plain increment is safe here, unlike the stock decrement, since there's no "insufficient balance" failure mode for *adding* points — Postgres's row-level lock on the UPDATE is sufficient) and logs the accrual `LoyaltyLedgerEntry`. `voidSale` claws back whatever points a voided sale had accrued, symmetric with the existing inventory-restock behavior — clamped at zero rather than blocking the void, in case the customer already redeemed those points elsewhere.

**UI**: new `components/retailer/pos/customer-panel.tsx` (phone lookup-or-create, notes, purchase habits, loyalty balance + redeem, purchase-limit bar) rendered above the cart in `register-panel.tsx`; each cart line gets a manual "Add discount" $ input — deliberately not an auto-apply/stacking rules engine (that's a materially bigger project, same "start simple, layer complexity later" scope cut already used for `SplitContract`/`ProductRejection`). A loyalty redemption is conceptually cart-level, but the schema only carries a discount per line for now — applied to whichever cart line has the most room to absorb it, with the redeem button disabled whenever the cart is empty (a real bug caught before shipping: without that guard, points could be deducted server-side with nowhere to apply the resulting discount).

**A real bug found during live browser verification, not caught by any automated test, fixed on the spot.** Completing an actual sale against the pre-existing seeded retailer threw `Unique constraint failed on the fields: (retailerId, saleNumber)`. Root cause: that retailer already had a `Sale` row from earlier in this project's history, created before `SaleCounter` existed (back when sale numbering used a `SELECT MAX(saleNumber)+1` pattern). The `SaleCounter` migration only created the table — it never backfilled a starting value for retailers with pre-existing sales, so `createSale`'s upsert took the "create at value=1" branch and collided with the sale that already had `saleNumber=1`. Every automated concurrency/money test always started from a brand-new retailer with zero prior sales, so this gap never surfaced there. Fixed with a small backfill migration (`prisma/migrations/20260818000000_backfill_sale_counter/`) that sets each retailer's counter to `MAX(saleNumber)` from their existing sales, idempotent via `ON CONFLICT ... DO UPDATE SET value = GREATEST(...)` — safe to re-run, and the right shape for this to also protect a real production rollout against the same class of pre-existing-data gap.

**Verified:** `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npx next build` clean. `npm test` — 31/31 passing, including new tests for purchase-habits math, discount line-total math (including clamping a discount that exceeds the line's value), loyalty accrual/clawback-on-void, phone normalization, and a loyalty-accrual concurrency test (scaled to 8 concurrent attempts after a higher number hit an unrelated Prisma Client connection-pool ceiling in this dev environment — a real, worth-noting environment constraint, not a correctness bug, documented inline in the test). Live in the running app: looked up a nonexistent phone number, created "Ronald Reagan" as a new customer, added Blue Dream to the cart, applied a $10 line discount (receipt correctly showed `$135.00 → $125.00`), hit the SaleCounter bug and fixed it live, completed the sale, and confirmed against the database that loyalty accrued exactly right (290 pts = 2 pts/$ × $145 total, one matching ledger entry) and the purchase-limit bar correctly showed `16.00 / 2.5 oz` with the over-limit warning after a 1 lb purchase.

Message format: `"{retailer's real business name} is running low on {product} ({category}) — consider offering a matching listing."`

Live: created a test Account Executive account, sold down a seeded lot from 8/10 to 2/10 (crossing the 0.2 threshold) via a real `createSale` call, confirmed exactly one notification landed for the AE with the correct retailer name and product, confirmed a second low-stock sale against the same already-flagged lot did *not* send a duplicate, and confirmed in the actual browser (logged in as the AE) that the notification bell renders the message correctly.

## 41. Cart orders, tiered pricing, and menu collections (added 2026-08-19)

**The core design decision: reuse the existing per-`(listing, retailer)` negotiation engine, don't replace it.** A "cart order" is not a second state machine — `lib/cart-orders.ts`'s `submitCartOrder` creates one ordinary `OfferThread` + initial `OfferRound` per selected product via the exact same `getOrCreateThread`/`addOfferRound` every other negotiation in this app already goes through, just tagging each resulting thread with a shared `OfferThread.cartOrderId` (new, nullable FK to a new lightweight `CartOrder` model) purely for grouping. Every line item still negotiates completely independently after that — a seller can accept one product from a cart submission, counter another, and reject a third, because each is just an ordinary thread. This directly answers this session's own earlier question #1 ("line-item response, not all-or-nothing?") — yes, by construction, since nothing about "it came from a cart" changes how `addOfferRound` behaves.

**Tiered pricing is per-listing and a soft threshold, not a hard reject.** `Listing.minimumOrderQuantity`/`belowMinimumPricePerUnit` (new, both nullable) — `pricePerUnit` applies at `minimumOrderQuantity` or more, `belowMinimumPricePerUnit` applies to anything smaller; both null means no tiering, same as before this existed. Set by the grower/processor on the existing `EditListingForm` (single-item mode only — bulk-import doesn't get these two fields in this pass, a deliberate scope cut, editable afterward from the listing's own edit form like anything else). `submitCartOrder` recomputes the effective price server-side from the listing's own stored tier rather than trusting whatever the client submitted, same posture as every other price-sensitive path in this app; a submission at exactly the tiered price auto-accepts (matching `isAsPosted`'s existing logic), anything else becomes an ordinary counter for the seller to review.

**A real bug this exposed and fixed: accepting used to close the WHOLE listing regardless of how much was actually accepted.** Before cart orders, nothing in this app's UI ever let a retailer request less than a listing's full posted quantity, so `Deal.finalQuantity` always equaled `Listing.quantity` and unconditionally closing the listing on accept (`lib/offers.ts`'s `addOfferRound`) was always correct. A cart order buying 20 lb out of a 500 lb listing broke that assumption — closing the whole listing after a 20 lb sale would have pulled the remaining 480 lb out of the marketplace. Fixed by computing `remainingQuantity = listing.quantity - finalQuantity` and only closing the listing when it hits zero, otherwise decrementing `Listing.quantity` and leaving it active. Safe for every pre-existing code path (finalQuantity always equals the full listing quantity there, so remainingQuantity is always exactly 0, same "close" behavior as before) — this new branch can only ever activate through the new cart flow.

**Menu collections — "an account rep has 14 menus from 14 different growers, all 14 can be grouped and shared with one link."** New `MenuCollection` model (`salesRepId`, `batchIds: String[]` — a plain array, not a join table, matching the same lightweight-grouping choice `Listing.batchId` itself already made rather than inventing two different patterns for the same idea). `lib/menu-collections.ts`'s `menusForCollectionPicker` lists every menu across the AE's own assigned accounts (§38's exclusive-assignment scoping applies here too — an AE can only ever bundle menus belonging to sellers actually assigned to them); `/sales/collections/new` is a checkbox picker gated behind the terms disclaimer (below); `publicCollectionView` aggregates every currently-shareable listing (`status: "active"` **and** `visibility: "all"`, same rule as `publicListingView`/`publicMenuView`) across every batch in the collection, grouped by the seller's `anonHandle` — **never by the seller's real internal id**, which would have been the exact class of RSC-payload identity leak §21 already found and fixed once in this app; caught and corrected during this build before it shipped, not after.

**The public cart page — `components/cart/cart-builder.tsx`, shared by both `/menu/[batchId]` (one seller) and the new `/collection/[id]` (possibly several)** — a quantity input per product, live tiered-price computation as the visitor types, a running total, and a terms selector defaulting to **cash**, with inline copy stating smaller orders typically need cash on delivery but a seller can agree to other terms as their own call. Same public/gated-action split as §36's listing page: anyone can browse and build a cart, but submitting requires a logged-in Retailer session (`lib/public-cart-actions.ts`'s `submitPublicCartOrder` resolves the retailer id from the verified session, never a client-supplied value, same authorization posture as `respondToListingAsRetailer`) — a logged-out visitor sees the same sign-in/sign-up links §36 already built, carrying the same `callbackUrl` pattern back to the cart page. **Deliberately not built in this pass: an Admin/AE-assisted cart submission** (§36's admin-assisted retailer flow has no cart-order equivalent yet) — a logged-out or wrong-role visitor just can't submit, matching scope to what was actually asked and tested.

**The AE liability disclaimer is a client-side gate at collection-creation time, not a database-tracked acceptance record** — `components/cart/terms-disclaimer.tsx` holds the draft text (same "not attorney-reviewed" posture as every other legal-adjacent surface in this app), shown on `/sales/collections/new` with a required checkbox before "Create link" enables, but not logged the way the non-circumvention agreement's `AgreementAcceptance` is. A deliberate lightweight choice for a first pass, not an oversight — `CartOrder.facilitatedBySalesRepId`/`disclaimerAcknowledgedAt` exist in the schema for a future AE-assisted-submission flow but are unused (always null) until that flow is actually built.

**The Account Executive gets notified when a grower approves non-cash terms** — a small addition to `addOfferRound`'s existing accept branch (`lib/offers.ts`): when a `Deal` closes with `finalTerms !== "cash"` and the listing has `createdBySalesRepId` set, that AE gets an in-app notification via the same `notify()` every other event in this app already uses. They're not a party to the deal and bear no responsibility for the terms (the disclaimer above), but they're the one who connected the parties, so this seemed worth telling them regardless.

**Also surfaced and fixed during this build, unrelated to cart orders**: `User.minimumOrderValue` (a per-grower dollar floor) was added in an earlier pass this same session before the human's worked example ("500 lb listed, 20 lb order, same or different price") made clear the real model is per-listing and a soft price tier, not a hard per-grower reject. Since it had shipped in a migration already applied to the live database but was never read by any committed code, it was cleanly dropped in a follow-up migration rather than left as a second, contradictory "minimum order" concept sitting unused in the schema.

**Verified:** `npx tsc --noEmit`, `npm run build`, full `npm test` (31/31) clean. Live end-to-end against the real database: created a 500 lb listing with a 50 lb tier (\$900/lb at 50+, \$1100/lb below), opened its public menu link logged out (correctly gated), signed in as a real retailer, requested 20 lb — confirmed the tiered \$1100/lb price computed live in the UI — and submitted; confirmed in the database the resulting `OfferThread` auto-accepted at exactly `price: 1100, quantity: 20`, a real `Deal` was created with matching `finalPrice`/`finalQuantity` and an auto-generated invoice, the `CartOrder` row linked correctly, and — the specific bug this session found — the listing stayed `status: "active"` with `quantity` correctly decremented from 500 to 480 rather than closing outright. ## 42. Cart UX fixes, broadcast-to-role sharing, Admin/AE feature parity, and order cancellation (added 2026-08-19)

Four follow-ups from the same conversation as §41, landed once real usage of the just-shipped cart/collection pages surfaced gaps.

**A. The cart page defaulted every product to 0, not the full amount offered — fixed, plus a real three-way Accept/Counter/Reject flow.** The human's report after actually receiving a real menu link: "the cart has 0 for each product it should display the full amount available as that is the wholesale offer to the retailer... retailer should see a display that says accept offer, counter offer or reject." `components/cart/cart-builder.tsx` was rewritten around that framing rather than patched:
- Every quantity input now initializes to the listing's own full `quantity` (`fullOfferQuantities`), not empty/0 — the page now reads as "here is the seller's actual offer," not a blank order form. A line that's been touched (quantity and/or price) gets an amber border and an inline note ("Adjusted from the full N lb offered" / "Price adjusted from the seller's $X/lb") so it's visually obvious which lines are still exactly as offered and which aren't.

**D. Canceling (`cancelCartOrder`) does not invent a new kind of authority: it finds every still-`open` thread in that order belonging to an account this actor actually has standing over, and calls the exact same `addOfferRound(..., action: "reject")` any seller-side rejection already goes through — **acting with the listing's own seller identity** (`actorRole: "seller"`, `actorId: listing.postedById`), the same "AE acts as the seller they're assigned to" posture already established for editing a listing on their behalf (§13/§38), not a new capability being invented for this feature specifically. A cart order spanning several sellers (e.g. from a multi-menu collection) only has its *own* lines canceled by a given actor — a line belonging to a different rep's account is left untouched, both in what's displayed and what a cancel action is allowed to touch. New `/sales/orders` and `/admin/orders` pages (`components/sales/orders-list.tsx` shared by both, `components/sales/cancel-order-button.tsx` for the confirm-then-cancel button) — the button calls `router.refresh()` after a successful cancel so the line's status badge updates immediately instead of showing stale "OPEN" until a manual reload, a real gap caught during live testing (the server action completes and the database is correct immediately, but a Server Component page has no reason to know to re-render on its own without that nudge).

## 43. Full-pipeline confirmation for cart orders, and bulk photo upload for an already-posted menu (added 2026-08-19)

**A. Confirmed, not assumed: a cart-order-originated offer flows through the entire pre-existing negotiation → fulfillment pipeline unchanged.** The human asked directly whether a grower still receives a cart-order line as a normal offer they can accept/counter/reject, and whether invoice → transporter assignment → delivery → product acceptance still all work for it. Rather than answer from memory, this was checked two ways: (1) grep confirmed `threadsForSeller`/`threadsForListing` (the grower's own negotiation dashboard queries) have zero reference to `cartOrderId` anywhere, and `acceptInvoiceAndAssignTransporter`/the rest of `lib/shipments.ts` operate purely on `dealId` with zero reference to `OfferThread` at all — so a `Deal`, once created, carries no trace of having come from a cart order. (2) A real one-off integration test (`lib/cart-orders.ts`'s `submitCartOrder` → grower's own `threadsForSeller` → `Deal` + auto-invoice → `acceptInvoiceAndAssignTransporter` → `advanceShipmentStatus` through `delivered` → `acceptProduct`) was written, run against the real database using the actual production functions, passed end to end, then deleted (not kept as a permanent suite addition — it was answering a one-time question, not testing new behavior). One terminology clarification given in the same answer: there is no single "transport rejection fee" — §14's rejection fee (negotiated by grower/retailer, triggers on retailer product rejection after delivery) and §18's transport fee (what the transporter charges, tracked not negotiated) are two separate, already-existing things; cart orders don't set a rejection-fee rate at submission, so one defaults to 0/none unless the parties separately negotiate it, identical to how a plain single-listing accept-as-posted already behaved.

**B. Bulk photo upload for a menu that's already posted — the deferred ask from earlier in this session, picked up now.** The gap, stated plainly by the human: bulk-imported menus (§34/§37) can attach a photo per row *at the time of the bulk paste*, and a single listing can always get a photo via its own edit form (§37), but there was no way to add photos to *several* products in an *already-posted* menu at once — you'd have to open each of (possibly 34) individual listings one at a time.
- **`lib/ai-listing.ts`'s `matchPhotosToMenu(filenames, strainNames)`** — a new, cheap **text-only** Claude call (no vision needed) that matches uploaded filenames against the menu's own product names, e.g. `"blue-dream-2.jpg"` → `"Blue Dream"`. Same "not configured" fallback and same "draft only, always reviewable" posture as `structureListingDraft(sBulk)` — nothing is assigned until the seller/AE confirms it in the review table, and every match is a plain `<select>` dropdown they can freely override.
- **`lib/listings.ts`'s `bulkAddMediaToMenu(batchId, callerId, assignments, opts)`** — the actual save. Re-validates every assignment server-side against the real `batchId` and the real per-listing authorization rule `updateListing` already enforces (seller who posted it, the AE/Admin who created it, or the AE currently assigned to that seller) — a file mistakenly aimed at a listing outside this menu, or one the caller has no standing over, is silently skipped rather than failing the whole batch, same "partial-but-explained beats all-or-nothing" posture as the bulk listing importer (§34). Verified with a real integration test (not the flaky browser this time — see the note below): photos landed on the right listings, a photo aimed at a listing in a *different* seller's *different* batch was correctly skipped and left that listing's media empty, `lastConfirmedAt` bumped on the listings that were actually touched, and a second grower with no relationship to the menu was correctly rejected outright.
- **`components/seller/bulk-photo-upload.tsx`** — pick several files at once, "Match with AI" pre-fills each row's dropdown, review/override, save. Wired into two places: `components/sales/menu-section.tsx` (already shared by the AE's and Admin's account pages, so both got this automatically — new `bulkPhotoSaveAction` prop, optional, since only those two pages pass one) and, new this pass, **the seller's own dashboard** (`components/seller/seller-dashboard.tsx`), which previously showed a flat ungrouped grid of listings — never grouped into menus at all, unlike the AE/Admin views §38–§40 already fixed for *their* side. Reused `groupListingsIntoMenus` (now generic over the listing shape via a type parameter, since the seller dashboard's own query includes `threads` alongside `media`, which the function's old fixed type didn't carry through) rather than duplicating the grouping logic a third time. New `components/seller/dashboard-menu-group.tsx` is a thin client wrapper owning just the collapse state and the upload panel — the existing per-listing card JSX stays exactly as it was, passed in as `children` from the server component. Defaults to **expanded**, deliberately different from `MenuSection`'s collapsed-by-default — the AE/Admin views collapse because they're managing many other people's menus at once; a seller looking at *their own* listings had never had anything hidden by default before this change, and this isn't the place to introduce that.
